Sleep EEG-fMRI: preprocessing, staging and replication

In progress

EEG recorded during fMRI is buried under scanner and heartbeat artefacts. The first study compares the same sleep recordings raw, filtered only and fully cleaned, and asks how much each step changes how well a model agrees with expert sleep scorers, while checking that cleaning keeps the brain signal it should.

5
pre-registered studies: 4 complete, 1 in progress
3
sites: Penn State, NIH and Geneva (OpenNeuro)
29
participants analysed so far, 34 more in the replication
  • Artefact removal: scanner gradient and heartbeat by average subtraction, two heartbeat detectors
  • Quality control with standard tools: PREP bad channels, Autoreject epochs, INPS, checksums
  • All 7 preprocessing steps covered, including an OBS test that was evaluated and not adopted
  • Features: spectral band power plus spindles, slow waves and alpha peak (YASA, AASM derivations)
  • Evaluation: leave-one-person-out, Cohen's kappa, paired Wilcoxon tests, bootstrap CIs
  • Reproducible: pre-registrations with dated amendments, corrections logs, verified references

Studies so far

  • 01 · Does cleaning matter? Complete. Cleaning did not significantly improve automated staging (Δκ = 0.06, p = 0.33). Exploratory analyses suggest that without it the classifier partly tracked heart rate, and that slow-wave detection was unusable on raw data
  • 01b · Classifying N1. Complete. No feature method improved the hardest stage significantly; tuning the decision threshold trades N1 for wake and N2 accuracy
  • 02 · Alpha before and after a task. Complete. Alpha frequency unchanged; alpha power higher in the second rest run (no control condition, so not attributed to the task)
  • 03 · Sleep across a session. Complete. Among the 14 participants selected as sleepers, sleep did not change significantly across runs, and even their "rest" runs were mostly sleep (a selection that depends on the outcome, stated in the study)
  • 04 · Does it replicate? In progress. Pre-registered 27 Oct 2026; independent raw data from NIH (overnight) and cleaned data from Geneva (nap). The pilot caught a scanner-artefact fault, fixed and verified before the full run

Current research

Is it the fly brain's wiring that sends each sense to the right place?

The complete wiring diagram of the adult fruit fly brain is now used as a ready-made network for simulations and AI models. I test whether its exact wiring, or just its connection statistics, decides where each sense reaches the neurons that drive the body. Every method is a published one. I set the questions and review every decision; the code and analyses are written with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code) under my direction.

Fruit fly connectome: sensory routing under null models

In progress

Seven senses are driven through a calibrated model of all 139,255 neurons, and the signal is read out on the 1,305 descending neurons that connect brain to body. The real brain is compared with randomised brains that keep every neuron's number of connections, including new randomisations that lock the brain's sensory and motor edges and shuffle only the wiring inside.

139k
neurons in the FlyWire v783 connectome, 2.7M connections
140
randomised brains simulated across four null models
99.8%
of descending neurons matched to the Stürner et al. 2025 atlas

Findings so far

  • The edges set the focus. Standard randomisation spreads each sense over 6–7× more descending neurons. Locking the sensory and descending connections and shuffling only the inside keeps ~99.5% of the focus (70 brains), consistent with Park 2026
  • The inside sets the destination. Simulated activity agrees with Stürner's structural map at ρ = 0.65 in the real brain, 0.51 after inside-only shuffling and 0.07 after standard shuffling; no randomised brain matches the real one (35 each)
  • Senses differ. Touch, which has direct routes, keeps its match; smell, humidity and photoreceptors, which pass through more inside layers, lose up to 40% of it
  • Reproduced first. The Shiu et al. 2024 whole-brain spiking model, r = 0.99 against the published rates
